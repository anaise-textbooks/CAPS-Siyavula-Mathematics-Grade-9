## Solving equations by inspection

2. Six equations are listed below the table. Use the table to
               find out for which of the given values of \(x\) it will be
               true that the left-hand side of the equation is equal to the
               right-hand side.
   <Callout type="note" title="Note">
   "Searching" for the solution of
                 an equation by using tables is called **solution by
                   inspection**.
   </Callout>
   | \(x\) | -3 | -2 | -1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | \(2x + 3\) | -3 | -1 | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 11 |
   | \(x + 4\) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
   | \(9 - x\) | 12 | 11 | 10 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 6 | 5 |
   | \(3x- 2\) | -11 | -8 | -5 | -2 | 1 | 4 | 7 | 10 |
   | \(10x - 7\) | -37 | -27 | -17 | -7 | 3 | 13 | 23 | 33 |
   | \(5x+ 3\) | -12 | -7 | -2 | 3 | 8 | 13 | 18 | 23 |
   | \(10 - 3x\) | 19 | 16 | 13 | 10 | 7 | 4 | 1 | -2 |
   2. \(2x+ 3 = 5x + 3\)
   4. \(5x + 3 = 9 -x\)
   6. \(2x+ 3 =x + 4 \)
   8. \(10x- 7 = 5x + 3\)
   10. \(3x - 2 = x + 4\)
   12. \( 9 - x = 2x + 3\)

Two equations can have the same
        solution. For example, \(5x= 10\) and \(x + 2 = 4\) have
        the same solution; \(x= 2\) is the solution for both
        equations.

<Callout type="note" title="Note">
Two equations are called
          **equivalent** if they have the same solution.
</Callout>
